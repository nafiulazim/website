Rich countries promised $100 billion. The new goal is $300 billion.

Ahead of COP31 in Antalya, here is what developed countries have delivered, what the next goals ask for, and who is still waiting for the money.

NABy Nafiul AzimInternational development professional
Data compiled 4 Nov 2026Climate finance
Share
$136.7Bprovided and mobilised by developed countries in 2024, above the old $100B goal
$300Ba year by 2035 is the new goal agreed at COP29, within a $1.3T ambition
$34.7Bof the 2024 total went to adaptation. Mitigation got $86.9B
7%of 2024 climate finance reached low-income countries ($9.6B)

The ladder of promises

US$ billion a year, drawn to scale

OECD 2026 · COP29 decision
Delivered, 2024$136.7B
Goal for 2035 (developed countries)$300B
Ambition for 2035 (all sources)$1.3T
The $100B goal was first met in 2022 ($115.9B), two years late. 2023 reached $132.8B and 2024 $136.7B. The 2035 goal is about 2.2 times the 2024 level.

Climate Policy Initiative estimates the world needs $6.3 trillion a year in climate finance for 2024–2030 to avoid the worst impacts.

Total tracked climate finance reached $1.9T in 2023 and early data show it passed $2T in 2024. Most of it is raised and spent inside countries, not sent from rich to poor countries.
