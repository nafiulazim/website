Child malnutrition: 150.2 million under-5s are stunted. In Africa, the number is still rising.

For World Children's Day on 20 November: how many young children are too short or too thin for their age, where they live, what it costs in lives, and how many children grow up in poverty.

NABy Nafiul AzimInternational development professional
Data compiled 18 Nov 2026Child malnutrition✎ Children & EducationGlobal
Share
Key takeaways
  1. 150.2 million children under 5 worldwide were stunted in 2024 (too short for their age, a sign of long-term undernutrition). That is 23.2% of young children, down from 26.4% in 2012.
  2. Africa is the only region where the number of stunted children under 5 has grown: from 61.7 million in 2012 to 64.8 million in 2024. Asia and Africa together are home to 94% of stunted young children.
  3. About 412 million children lived on less than US$3 a day in 2024 (World Bank and UNICEF). Separately, UNICEF counts 417 million children in low- and middle-income countries severely deprived in at least two basics, such as nutrition or sanitation.
