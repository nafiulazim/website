Extreme weather killed 832,000 people in 30 years. 6 of the 10 hardest-hit countries are lower-middle-income.

As COP31 meets in Antalya, here is who extreme weather has hit hardest over three decades, how many people it forces from home, and what it costs women and health.

NABy Nafiul AzimInternational development professional
Data compiled 11 Nov 2026Climate disasters🌿 Climate & EnvironmentGlobal
Share
Key takeaways
  1. Extreme weather (storms, floods, heat waves and more) killed more than 832,000 people worldwide in 1995–2024 and caused over US$4.5 trillion in direct losses, according to the Germanwatch Climate Risk Index.
  2. Of the 10 countries hit hardest by extreme weather in 1995–2024, 6 are lower-middle-income. Dominica, Myanmar and Honduras top the list.
  3. In rural areas of 24 low- and middle-income countries, households headed by women lose 8% more of their income to heat stress and 3% more to floods each year than households headed by men (FAO).
